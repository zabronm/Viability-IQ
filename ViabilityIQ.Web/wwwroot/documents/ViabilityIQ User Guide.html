Viability.IQ Complete User Guide

A practical guide to administering a workspace, setting up core information, completing financial assessments, analysing results and operating the application safely.

Viability.IQ supports subscription-based workspaces in which authorised users maintain clients and businesses, prepare 12-month assessments, review financial viability, test scenarios and create management budgets. Features shown to each user depend on the subscription, active tenant membership, assigned Site, tenant roles and record permissions.

SectionWho should use itPurpose
Section A — Subscription & System AdministrationSubscribers, Tenant Owners, Tenant Administrators and authorised operational staffRegister, manage access, configure master data, create clients/businesses, use notifications and run portfolio reports.
Section B — Working with an AssessmentAdvisors, analysts, Site Managers, reviewers and authorised viewersCapture evidence, produce projections, review dashboards/reports, test sensitivity and prepare a budget.
Section C — Secure Operation & SupportAll users and support administratorsUnderstand session recovery, inactivity, database availability, safe working practices and troubleshooting.
Professional-use notice: Viability.IQ supports assessment and management decisions. It does not replace audited accounts, contractual records, tax advice or professional judgement, and it does not guarantee future performance.

Section A — Subscription & System Administration

Set up the workspace in the correct order so that the Company, Regions, Sites, users, master data, clients, businesses and assessments are linked consistently.

1.1 Plans, registration and seats

A new subscriber registers at /identity/register, chooses a plan, completes the Company and Tenant & Owner tabs, accepts the terms and creates the first tenant workspace. The Tenant is the security and subscription boundary; the Company is the subscribing organisation represented inside that boundary.

ConceptUser meaning
Standard planA single-seat Company workspace suitable for an owner-managed organisation.
Open planA Company workspace intended for multiple users, Sites and role-controlled collaboration.
TenantThe private security, data-isolation and subscription boundary. It owns the Company profile, membership, roles and operational records.
CompanyThe subscribing organisation profile. Each Tenant has exactly one Company.
RegionA tenant-owned geographical or operational grouping belonging to the Company. It is not global shared master data.
SiteA tenant-owned operating location belonging to one Region. Users are assigned to Sites, and their Region is derived from the selected Site.
SeatOne active tenant membership. Suspended or removed memberships do not consume an active seat.
Subscription statusControls whether the tenant can continue using subscribed functionality. Renewal and upgrade actions are handled by the authorised account owner or administrator.
  1. Choose the plan appropriate to the number of expected users and administration needs.
  2. On the Company tab, enter the organisation identity, registration, contact and address information.
  3. On the Tenant & Owner tab, enter the initial Region, initial Site, seat requirement and owner credentials.
  4. Submit registration. Provisioning creates the Tenant, Company, initial Region, initial Site, subscription, owner membership and protected Tenant Owner role together.
  5. After registration, sign in and verify the Company, Site, plan and owner role.
Provisioning integrity: if the tenant structure cannot be provisioned successfully, registration is treated as unsuccessful rather than intentionally leaving a usable account without its Company, Region, Site or membership.

1.2 Signing in, password recovery and signing out

  • Sign in: open Login, enter the registered email and password, and select Sign In.
  • Temporary password: a newly invited user must change the temporary password before entering normal application routes.
  • Forgotten password: use Forgot Password, follow the reset message, and choose a password that satisfies the displayed policy.
  • Sign out: open the profile menu and select Sign Out. Always sign out on a shared computer.
  • Inactive session: the default session warns after 25 inactive minutes and signs out at 30 minutes.
Home page context: after sign-in, verify the displayed user, Tenant, Site, last login and active assessment before processing records.

1.3 User Profile component and Activity Log

The avatar in the application header opens the User Profile menu. It displays the signed-in user, highest-ranked role and any additional roles.

Menu itemFunction

Account SettingsReview subscription dates and configure the assigned Site, interface and notification preferences. Region is derived from Site.

The Activity Log is evidence of recorded application actions; it is not an editable transaction screen. Report incorrect entries to an administrator rather than attempting to alter history.

1.4 Account settings

Account Settings contains read-only subscription information and user-maintained preferences.

  • Subscription & Licensing: plan, registration date and expiry date.
  • Site Allocation: select an active Site belonging to the Tenant. The related Region is displayed or saved automatically and cannot be independently paired with another Site.
  • Interface Preferences: theme and language options where supported.
  • Notification Channels: email, SMS and phone-call preferences. Availability also depends on configured delivery services.

Select Save Settings and wait for success confirmation before leaving the page.

2.2 Tenant roles and access

RoleTypical responsibility

A user may hold more than one role. Access is the combined result of tenant membership, membership status, subscription status, Site context, roles and any explicit record grants. Assign the least privilege needed.

2.3 Adding and managing users

  1. Open Settings > Users.
  2. Select Add and enter the user’s name, email, Site and required role or roles.
  3. The Site must belong to the active Tenant. Its Region is derived automatically; users cannot be assigned an unrelated Region/Site combination.
  4. Save. For a new identity, the system creates a secure temporary password and attempts to email it. An existing Viability.IQ identity is linked to the tenant without creating a duplicate account.
  5. Ask the user to sign in and change a temporary password immediately.
  6. Return to Users to change roles, default Site or membership status when responsibilities change.

Use Settings > User Groups / Roles to review role assignments and protected access profiles. Maintain at least one active Tenant Owner.

2.4 Sites, membership status and security

  • Suspend to block access temporarily while retaining history.
  • Reactivate only after confirming the user still requires access and a seat is available.
  • Remove the tenant membership when access must end; historical audit references remain.
  • Default Site determines the user’s normal operating context. Confirm it after saving and after the user signs in again.
  • Derived Region comes from the Site and must not be independently changed to create a mismatched allocation.
  • Tenant ownership prevents a Region or Site from another Tenant being assigned to the membership.
  • Explicit grants may permit View, Edit or Delete for selected records but never override tenant isolation.

3.1 Recommended system setup order

  1. Confirm the single Company / Organisation Profile created for the Tenant.
  2. Confirm at least one tenant-owned Region and Site. Add further Regions and Sites before assigning users to them.
  3. Assign each user to the correct Site and least-privilege tenant role or roles.
  4. Create shared classification data needed by forms.
  5. Create the client or business owner/contact.
  6. Create the business and link it to the correct client and classifications.
  7. Create the assessment for that business and set its assessment period.
  8. Open the assessment and complete assumptions before detailed monthly data.
Avoid duplicates: search existing lists before adding a client, business, product/service, funder or classification. Inactivate obsolete records instead of creating slightly different spellings.

3.3 Creating clients and businesses

Clients

  1. Open Settings > Clients.
  2. Add the person or organisation contact, select the appropriate category, and capture reliable communication details.
  3. Save and confirm the record appears once in the list.

Businesses

  1. Open Settings > Businesses.
  2. Add the legal/trading identity and link the correct client, sector and tenant-owned regional information.
  3. Save, then open the record to verify the relationship before creating an assessment.

3.4 Creating and setting up an assessment

  1. Open My Assessments and select Add.
  2. Select the business. The case number is system-generated.
  3. Enter the assessment start date, optional finish date and useful internal remarks.
  4. Keep the assessment active while it is available for processing.
  5. Save, then select the case number to initialise the assessment workspace.

Do not create a second assessment merely because data entry is incomplete. Continue the existing active case unless a genuinely separate review period or assignment is required.

Section B — Working with an Assessment

Viability.IQ’s financial engine joins the information entered across the assessment into one month-by-month business picture. Sales influence cash receipts, debtors, stock, cost of Sales and VAT; expenses, assets, loans and creditor timing influence payments, profit, cash and the balance sheet. The engine then presents the same underlying projection through dashboards and formal reports. Sensitivity Analysis lets the user test “what if” changes without altering the approved source data, while Budgeting converts the projection into a separate management plan. Because the areas are connected, accurate source information and a readiness review are more important than manually adjusting a final report.

6.1 Opening and navigating an assessment

  1. Open My Assessments.
  2. Select the case number. Confirm the case, business, client and period in the assessment header.
  3. Use the assessment navigation for Assumptions, Dashboards, Assets, Sales/Income, Debtors/Creditors, Stock, Expenses, Loans, VAT, Reports, Sensitivity Analysis and Budgets.
  4. Watch the status indicators: a warning means that area has not yet been populated or verified.

Browser Back, Forward and Refresh can recreate the interactive session. The application restores the active tenant and assessment only after checking access again; always confirm the displayed case before continuing.

6.2 Assessment readiness, review and closing

StatusMeaning
DraftThe case exists but material assessment work has not started.
In ProgressData is being captured and readiness is developing.
Ready for ReviewRequired readiness checks pass and the case has been submitted for review.
CompletedAn authorised reviewer has finalised the assessment. Completion details are recorded.
ArchivedThe case is retained for history but no longer active operational work.
  1. Select View Readiness and resolve all blocking items.
  2. Select Submit for Review. The system re-evaluates readiness.
  3. Review dashboards, source data, assumptions and formal reports.
  4. Select Mark Complete only when the status is Ready for Review and the review is finished.
  5. If an authorised correction is later required, use Re-open; the case returns to In Progress and completion details are cleared.

7. Assumptions and Projection Readiness

Assumptions define the opening position and the expected behaviour that cannot be derived reliably from monthly records alone.

AreaWhat to confirm
Opening positionOpening bank, stock, debtors, creditors, VAT, other current assets/liabilities and asset balances agree to supporting records.
SalesGrowth rate/start month, cash Sales share, income classification and fallback cost of Sales are reasonable.
Debtors and creditorsAgeing distribution, payment timing, bad debt and VAT inclusion reflect actual trading behaviour.
Expenses and wagesExpense escalation, contingency, director numbers, wages and increase timing are supported.
VATRegistration status, rate, payment frequency and opening payable/refundable balance are correct.
Funding and assetsLoan terms, minimum cash reserve, acquisition timing and depreciation inputs agree to contracts/registers.

Percentage mode captures ageing shares. Actual values mode captures known balances and converts them to projection percentages.

Use assumption notes to record source, date, owner and reasoning. Confirmation records that the baseline was reviewed; it is not a guarantee.

Readiness result: Incomplete means a required item is missing; Ready with warnings means core information exists but advisory issues remain; Ready means minimum automated checks pass.

8. Assessment dashboards

Dashboards present the live 12-month projection as decision-focused summaries. Review:

  • KPI cards for revenue, gross profit, operating result, closing cash and related indicators.
  • Monthly profit/loss and cash trends rather than relying only on annual totals.
  • Business health and performance alerts, especially the first month of negative cash.
  • Working-capital distribution and debtor/creditor timing.
  • Financial viability indicators and explanatory ratios.

A green indicator is not proof that every source record is correct. Return to source pages when a value looks unexpected; do not compensate by entering an unrelated adjustment.

9.1 Asset management

  1. Add each asset with its category/type, opening cost, accumulated depreciation, acquisition date/month, depreciation method/rate and active status.
  2. Use bulk import only after checking the template, classifications and duplicate risk.
  3. Open asset details to review opening value, monthly movements, depreciation and monthly closing value.
  4. Add movements such as acquisitions, improvements, disposals or corrections in the month they occur.
  5. Deactivate an asset only when it should no longer participate in the active projection; retain records needed for history.

Months before a planned acquisition do not carry the asset. The acquisition month introduces the value and depreciation follows the configured treatment.

9.2 Sales and other income

  1. Create Sales Categories and link the correct income type and product/service category.
  2. Classify true Sales separately from grants, donations, sundry income and disposal proceeds.
  3. Enter monthly Sales/income for Months 1–12 and save before leaving the page.
  4. Review category markup/cost basis, cash Sales percentage and opening stock relationship.
Classification matters: only true Sales should drive gross profit, debtors, stock, cost of Sales and break-even calculations. Do not classify a grant or asset disposal as Sales merely to increase revenue.

9.3 Debtors and creditors management

Debtors control when credit Sales become cash. Creditors control when supplier purchases become cash payments.

  • Use ageing percentages when reliable collection/payment patterns are known.
  • Use actual values when an aged ledger is available; the application derives percentages.
  • Confirm whether captured balances include VAT.
  • Review bad debt separately from timing: bad debt reduces collectible Sales; timing only shifts collection.
  • Check that total percentages equal 100% and that average days are commercially plausible.

9.4 Stock management

  1. Capture opening stock by the relevant Sales/Product category rather than as an unsupported duplicate total.
  2. Enter monthly stock movements or requirements using the available assessment form.
  3. Maintain a reasonable minimum closing-stock level where the business carries inventory.
  4. Review purchases, cost of Sales and closing stock together; unexplained stock growth can overstate assets and understate cost.

9.5 Expense management

  1. Select standard expense items or create authorised master data first.
  2. Enter monthly amounts, VAT treatment, recurrence and applicable increase timing.
  3. Use bulk entry/import carefully and review every mapped item before saving.
  4. Keep director wages and known recurring costs explicit; do not hide known expenses in contingency.

Wait for the success Toast after saving. Reopen or refresh the page to confirm material entries persisted.

9.6 Loans and repayments

  1. Add each facility with funder, loan type, opening/new balance, interest rate, term, start month and contractual instalment.
  2. Review the generated repayment schedule and edit a movement only when supported by the agreement.
  3. Distinguish principal repayments from interest: principal reduces the liability; interest affects expense and cash.
  4. Record new facilities in the correct month so proceeds and repayment timing are not shifted.

9.7 VAT management

The VAT workspace combines taxable Sales, purchases/stock and VAT-bearing expenses to estimate output VAT, input VAT and settlement.

  • Confirm the VAT rate and payment frequency in Assumptions.
  • Review opening VAT payable or refundable and ensure only one opening position is used.
  • Enter supported VAT adjustments in the relevant month with a clear reason.
  • Review the projected settlement months and compare them with filed returns before relying on cashflow.

11. Sensitivity Analysis and GroqCloud AI

Sensitivity Analysis tests alternative conditions without changing the assessment’s saved source records.

Deterministic analysis

  • Scenario Builder: combine changes to Sales, costs, expenses, bad debt, collection/payment timing, loan interest or asset acquisition.
  • Sensitivity Results: vary one driver across a range to identify the most influential assumption.
  • Scenario Comparison: compare the baseline with up to three named cases.
  • Risk & Break-even: estimate practical thresholds and cash-reserve pressure.
StatusMeaning
GreenNo tested key threshold is breached.
AmberA cash reserve or coverage warning requires investigation.
RedThe tested case produces negative cash or a projected loss.

GroqCloud AI analysis

  1. Open the GroqCloud AI tab. The server must show the provider as enabled and configured.
  2. Read the disclosure. Only de-identified calculated results are sent; names and raw source records should not be included.
  3. Give explicit consent for the current assessment session.
  4. Select Generate GroqCloud Analysis and review the executive summary and findings.
  5. Verify every recommendation against source records, deterministic calculations and professional judgement.
  6. Use Clear to remove the current in-memory result.
AI limitations: GroqCloud output is advisory, may be incomplete or wrong, and is not saved as the authoritative assessment. Consent and generated output apply to the current session. Never put the API key in source control.

12. Assessment Budget

The budget is a separate 12-month management plan created from a snapshot of the assessment projection.

  1. Complete material assessment data and review readiness.
  2. Open Budgets and select Create from assessment projection.
  3. Enter a meaningful name and planning notes.
  4. Edit white monthly fields and select Save draft.
  5. Use Budget, Projection and Variance views to compare management targets with the imported assessment snapshot.
  6. If source data changes while the budget is Draft, use Refresh projection deliberately; review resulting variances again.
  7. Submit the budget for approval and approve only through an authorised role.
StatusEffect
DraftEditable and refreshable.
SubmittedRead-only while awaiting approval.
ApprovedLocked as the approved management plan.

Review monthly gross profit, EBITDA/operating result, profit before tax, funding shortfalls, loss-making months and material differences from the projection.

Section C — Secure Operation & Support

Protect the signed-in session, understand recovery behaviour and provide useful information when requesting support.

13. Sessions, browser recovery and database availability

Connection recovery

Blazor Server keeps an interactive browser connection. A brief interruption displays the reconnect overlay. Wait for reconnection; use Reload only when automatic recovery fails. A disconnected circuit is retained for a limited period.

Protected context

The minimum active context—user, tenant membership and assessment identifiers—is protected per browser tab. On a new circuit, the application reloads records and checks authorisation before restoring them. Another tab has independent context.

Inactivity

Mouse, keyboard, touch and scroll activity reset the inactivity timer. At the configured timeout, the application securely removes the authentication cookie and returns to Login. Unsaved form input is not preserved.

Database availability

  • During startup, temporary database failures are retried.
  • The web host displays a branded HTTP 503 page instead of leaving the browser apparently hanging.
  • After final failure, normal routes remain blocked and the page shows a safe error reference; support details remain in Serilog.
  • During normal use, safe timeout/unavailable messages provide references without exposing connection details.

14. Troubleshooting and support

SymptomRecommended response
Wrong Tenant, Site or caseStop entry, verify the header/profile context, return to the relevant list and reopen the correct record. Sign out/in after an authorised Site change.
Save appears unsuccessfulWait for the success Toast, check validation messages, refresh and verify the record. Do not repeatedly submit a financial transaction when the result is uncertain.
Database Timeout / Temporarily UnavailableWait briefly, retry once where safe, record the error reference and time, and contact support if repeated.
Connection cannot be restoredSelect Reload. The application will restore only context that remains authorised.
Session expiredSign in again, reopen the case and verify whether the previous action had a success Toast.

When escalating, provide the time, signed-in user, Tenant, Site, case number, page/action, displayed reference and whether the operation was retried. Never send passwords, connection strings, API keys or unredacted sensitive records.

15. Operational checklists

Before starting an assessment

  1. Confirm subscription and user access are active.
  2. Confirm Site, client and business records.
  3. Collect bank statements, management accounts, VAT returns, aged ledgers, stock count, asset register and loan statements.
  4. Create one assessment for the intended business and period.

Before submitting for review

  1. Resolve all required readiness checks.
  2. Reconcile opening balances and income classification.
  3. Review monthly Sales, expenses, stock, assets, loans and VAT.
  4. Review minimum cash month, warnings and dashboard trends.
  5. Generate all five assessment reports and investigate inconsistencies.
  6. Run at least one downside sensitivity scenario.
  7. Document material assumptions, evidence and mitigations.

Before marking complete

  1. Confirm the assessment is Ready for Review.
  2. Confirm reviewer comments and corrections are complete.
  3. Confirm exported or emailed reports use the correct period and recipients.
  4. Mark Complete and verify the completion status.
