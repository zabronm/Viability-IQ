Assessment Assumptions & Baseline

Practical guidance for preparing a credible 12-month SME projection.

This module brings together the minimum facts and assumptions needed to interpret Sales, costs, working capital, funding and assets consistently. It supports government-programme assessment and management discussion; it is not a substitute for audited accounts or professional tax advice.

How to use it: capture verified opening facts first, enter the most reasonable baseline assumptions, attach or reference supporting evidence, review the readiness checklist, and confirm the baseline before using it for decisions or sensitivity analysis.

1. Opening Position

SettingHow it supports projections
Opening stock / current assetsEstablishes the stock or current-asset value carried into Month 1 and supports working-capital and balance-sheet calculations.
Opening bank balanceSets the initial cash available. Every projected receipt and payment builds from this amount.
Overdraft interestProvides the annual financing rate applied where projected cash becomes overdrawn.
Projected depreciationProvides the opening depreciation allowance used where detailed asset calculations do not supply the complete amount.

Evidence: recent bank statement, stock count, management accounts, aged debtors/creditors, asset register and loan statements. A genuine zero should be confirmed rather than left unknown.

2. Sales Categories

SettingProjection use
Category and income typeSeparates true Sales from sundry income, grants, disposals and other income. Only true Sales should drive gross profit, stock, debtors and break-even measures.
MarkupSupports the category cost-of-Sales estimate and gross-margin calculation when purchase/stock information is incomplete.
Opening stockAssigns starting stock to the relevant Sales Category and feeds opening working capital.

3. Debtors & Creditors Profiles

Percentage mode Enter the expected distribution across ageing buckets. The 0–30 day bucket balances the total to 100%.

Actual values mode Enter known Rand balances such as R90,000 in 0–30 days and R35,000 in 31–60 days. The application converts each bucket to a percentage of the total and saves those percentages for projections.

6. Funding, Cash Reserve & Assets

Loan balance, rate, term and instalmentDrive financing balances, interest expense and monthly repayment cash outflows. The loan start month determines when a new facility begins affecting the projection.
Minimum cash balanceRepresents management’s preferred liquidity reserve. Falling below it should trigger a warning, not be treated as additional spending capacity.

7. Readiness, Notes & Confirmation

The readiness score checks whether essential opening facts, Sales Categories, monthly Sales, working-capital profiles, expenses, VAT, loans, asset depreciation and baseline assumptions are available. Incomplete means a required check failed. Ready with warnings means core data is available but an advisory item remains. Ready means the minimum automated checks pass.

Assumption notes should record the source, date, owner and reason for each material estimate—for example “based on signed orders” or “average of the last six VAT returns.”

Confirmation records that the baseline has been reviewed and is reasonable for the assessment. It does not prove that the forecast will occur.

Important cautions: avoid double-counting assumptions already represented by monthly source data; use fallback cost of Sales only when required; distinguish Sales from grants or disposals; keep VAT treatment consistent; and rerun readiness after changing material source data.

Recommended review sequence

  1. Verify the opening date and balances.
  2. Check income classification and monthly Sales.
  3. Confirm debtor, creditor and stock behavior.
  4. Review expenses, VAT and wages.
  5. Reconcile loans and assets to supporting statements.
  6. Review warnings, document sources and confirm the baseline.
