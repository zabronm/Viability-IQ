Viability.IQ Assessment, Budgeting, Tenant Administration & Session Resilience

Practical guidance for registration, tenant administration, secure session use and preparing a credible 12-month SME projection and budget.

This guide explains registration and subscription workspaces, tenant user administration, secure session behaviour, assessment baseline preparation, Sensitivity Analysis and assessment-linked budgeting. It supports government-programme assessment and management discussion; it is not a substitute for audited accounts, professional tax advice or a guarantee of future performance.

Recommended sequence: capture verified opening facts, enter reasonable baseline assumptions, review readiness, confirm the baseline, test uncertainty with Sensitivity Analysis, and then create the assessment budget for management approval and monitoring.

7. Readiness, Notes & Confirmation

The readiness score checks whether essential opening facts, Sales Categories, monthly Sales, working-capital profiles, expenses, VAT, loans, asset depreciation and baseline assumptions are available. Incomplete means a required check failed. Ready with warnings means core data is available but an advisory item remains. Ready means the minimum automated checks pass.

Assumption notes should record the source, date, owner and reason for each material estimate—for example, “based on signed orders” or “average of the last six VAT returns.”

Confirmation records that the baseline has been reviewed and is reasonable for the assessment. It does not prove that the forecast will occur.

Important cautions: avoid double-counting assumptions already represented by monthly source data; use fallback cost of Sales only when required; distinguish Sales from grants or disposals; keep VAT treatment consistent; and rerun readiness after changing material source data.

8. Using Sensitivity Analysis

Sensitivity Analysis tests how the approved 12-month projection responds when important assumptions change. It does not edit Sales, expenses, assets, loans or any other source record. Every analysis begins with a fresh copy of the authoritative baseline and applies temporary adjustments in memory.

Purpose: use the module to understand downside risk, test opportunities, estimate funding pressure and explain which assumptions have the greatest influence on viability. It should support professional judgement rather than replace it.

8.1 Before starting

  1. Open the correct assessment and confirm that its opening position and monthly source data are current.
  2. Review Projection Readiness and resolve material missing information.
  3. Confirm that the baseline projection is reasonable before testing alternatives.
  4. Select Sensitivity Analysis immediately after Assessment Reports in the assessment menu.

If Sales or expense information is incomplete, the page displays a Draft baseline warning. Analysis can still be run, but its conclusions must be treated as provisional.

8.2 Scenario Builder

The Scenario Builder combines one or more changes into a practical alternative case. Start with a preset or enter a name, description and individual adjustments.

Scenario presets

8.3 Understanding sensitivity drivers

DriverMeaning and interpretation
Sales / revenueChanges projected true Sales by the selected percentage. A value of −10% tests Sales that are 10% below baseline.
Cost of Sales — relativeChanges baseline cost of Sales by a percentage. A value of +10% makes the existing cost amount 10% higher.
Cost of Sales — percentage pointsChanges cost of Sales as a share of revenue. If baseline cost is 60% of Sales, +5 percentage points tests 65%.
Operating expensesChanges recurring operating expenses by the entered percentage for the selected period.
Bad debtAdds percentage points to the uncollectible portion of Sales and reduces expected customer receipts.
Debtor collection timingMoves projected customer receipts by whole months. A positive value delays receipts; a negative value accelerates them.
Creditor payment timingMoves projected supplier payments by whole months. A positive value delays payment; consider whether suppliers would permit it.
Loan interestChanges projected loan-interest expense by a relative percentage. It does not amend contractual principal repayments.
Asset purchasesChanges baseline projected asset-purchase cash outflows by the entered percentage.
Minimum cash reserveSets the management buffer used to identify funding shortfalls. It is a risk threshold, not an additional expense.
Percentage versus percentage points: these are different. Increasing a 60% cost-of-Sales ratio by 10% produces 66%; increasing it by 10 percentage points produces 70%.

8.4 Reading Scenario Builder results

Profit before tax (PBT)Profit after operating expenses, depreciation and interest, before tax.

The deterministic explanation lists the largest adjustments and the resulting movement in profit and closing cash. Expand Calculation scope to review modelling limitations.

8.5 Sensitivity Results

This tab changes one driver at a time while holding all others at baseline. It is the best place to identify which assumption has the greatest effect.

  1. Select the driver.
  2. Enter the minimum and maximum adjustment.
  3. Enter the step between test points. For example, −30 to +20 with a step of 5 tests −30%, −25%, −20% and so on.
  4. Select the effective month and choose Run range.
  5. Compare the baseline row with each test point and review the status colour and warning flags.

The chart shows closing cash across the range. The table adds revenue, gross margin, EBITDA, PBT, minimum cash, funding gap, current ratio and interest cover. Use CSV to download the detailed results for further review.

8.6 Scenario Comparison

This tab compares the baseline with up to three named scenarios. Select the scenarios to display their KPI variances and M1–M12 revenue, PBT and closing-cash movements.

  • Review amount and percentage variances, not only final totals.
  • Identify the first month in which cash or profit becomes unacceptable.
  • Compare the cause descriptions to distinguish operational risk from financing risk.
  • Export the comparison to CSV when the evidence must be shared or retained.

8.7 Risk & Break-even

Enter management’s minimum cash reserve and recalculate. The module progressively tests the baseline to estimate practical limits.

ThresholdInterpretation
Maximum Sales declineThe first tested decline that produces a loss or breaches the selected cash reserve.
Maximum operating-expense increaseThe first tested increase that produces a loss or breaches the reserve.
Minimum gross marginAn approximate annual margin required to cover operating expenses, depreciation and interest.
Collection-delay toleranceThe first whole-month customer-payment delay that breaches the reserve.
Minimum projected cashThe lowest monthly cash balance in the baseline and the month in which it occurs.

Findings are prioritised and paired with a practical management action. They are prompts for investigation and must be considered together with contracts, security, funding terms and management capacity.

8.8 Status colours

StatusMeaningSuggested response
GreenNo tested key threshold is breached.Continue monitoring and retain contingency plans.
AmberA cash reserve or coverage warning exists.Investigate the affected month and agree a mitigation before approval.
RedThe scenario produces negative cash or a projected loss.Do not rely on the case without corrective action, additional funding or revised assumptions.

8.9 Printing and exports

Select Print / Save PDF to open the browser print dialogue and save the active analysis view as a PDF. The Sensitivity Results and Scenario Comparison tabs also provide CSV exports. Record the assessment, baseline date, scenario name, adjustments and calculation date when sharing results.

8.10 Important calculation limitations

  • Scenarios are held in memory for the current page session and are not saved to the database.
  • The baseline remains unchanged; update source data on the appropriate assessment page when a change is intended to become permanent.
  • Existing VAT schedules remain at baseline during sensitivity overlays.
  • Collection or supplier payments shifted beyond Month 12 fall outside the analysis horizon.
  • Asset-purchase sensitivity changes the projected purchase cash outflow; it does not rebuild a new detailed asset register or depreciation schedule.
  • Thresholds are tested estimates and may be rounded to the selected increment or whole month.
  • Sensitivity results show what could happen under stated assumptions; they do not assign probabilities or predict which scenario will occur.

8.11 Recommended sensitivity review

  1. Run the baseline and confirm that no material data is missing.
  2. Run a Sales range to understand demand risk.
  3. Run cost-of-Sales and operating-expense ranges to understand margin pressure.
  4. Test slower collections and review the minimum-cash month.
  5. Run the Combined Downside preset and adapt it to the enterprise.
  6. Compare baseline, optimistic and pessimistic cases.
  7. Set the required cash reserve and review risk thresholds.
  8. Document management actions, supporting evidence and unresolved uncertainties.
  9. Export or print the final analysis for the assessment record.

9. Using the Assessment Budget

The Budgeting module converts the current assessment projection into a controlled 12-month management budget. Every budget belongs to one assessment and uses the same Month 1–12 period and assessment start date.

Important distinction: the assessment projection is the source and comparison baseline. The budget is a separate planning record. Editing the budget does not change Sales, expenses, stock, assets, loans, VAT, cash-flow calculations or any other assessment source record.

9.1 Before creating a budget

  1. Open the correct assessment and verify the case number displayed in the assessment layout.
  2. Complete the material Sales, stock, expense, asset, loan, VAT and working-capital information.
  3. Review Projection Readiness and resolve material missing data.
  4. Generate the assessment reports and confirm that projected profit and cash flow are reasonable.
  5. Use Sensitivity Analysis where appropriate to understand the main risks before committing to targets.

A budget may still be created from incomplete assessment information, but the imported amounts will reflect those omissions. Correct the assessment first whenever the missing information is material.

9.2 Opening the module

  1. Open the required assessment.
  2. Select the shortcuts menu in MainLayout_Assessment.
  3. Select Budgets — Assessment Budget & Variance.
  4. The application opens the budget belonging to that assessment. A budget from another assessment cannot be displayed through this route.

9.3 Creating the first budget

Each assessment supports one active budget. If no budget exists, enter a meaningful budget name, add optional planning notes, and select Create from assessment projection.

ViabilityIQ copies the assessment's current 12-month projection into two sets of values:

ValuePurpose
ProjectionA dated snapshot of the assessment result used as the comparison baseline.
BudgetThe management target. It initially equals the projection and can then be edited independently.

The snapshot includes revenue, other income, cost of goods sold, operating expenses, depreciation, interest, receipts, supplier payments, VAT payments, loan payments, asset purchases and calculated profit and cash-flow totals.

9.4 Editing the monthly planning matrix

Editable rows contain white monthly input fields. Enter the planned amount for each Month 1–12. Amounts are displayed to two decimal places.

SectionTypical editable linesCalculated lines
Profit & LossRevenue, other income, cost of goods sold, operating expenses, depreciation and interest expense.Gross profit, EBITDA, EBIT and profit before tax.
Cash FlowSales receipts, other receipts, supplier payments, cash operating expenses, VAT payments, loan payments and asset purchases.Total inflows, total outflows, net cash movement and closing cash balance.

Calculated rows cannot be entered manually. They update automatically when an editable amount changes and are recalculated again by the server when the draft is saved.

Profit and cash are different: changing budgeted revenue does not automatically change Sales receipts. Likewise, depreciation affects profit but is not a cash payment. Review both sections and keep related assumptions consistent.

9.5 Reading the three display modes

ModeMeaningRecommended use
BudgetShows the current management targets. Editable lines can be changed while the budget is Draft.Prepare and revise the budget.
ProjectionShows the most recently imported assessment projection snapshot.Confirm the financial model on which the budget was based.
VarianceShows Budget minus Projection for each month and total.Identify deliberate departures from the assessment result and document the reason.

A positive variance means the budget amount is higher than the projection; a negative variance means it is lower. Whether that is favourable depends on the line. Higher revenue may be favourable, while higher costs or payments may be unfavourable.

9.6 KPI cards and alerts

IndicatorInterpretation
Budget revenueTotal budgeted true revenue for Months 1–12 and its difference from projection.
Profit before taxBudgeted profit after operating costs, depreciation and interest, before tax.
Net cash movementTotal budgeted cash inflows less cash outflows over the 12 months.
Closing cashMonth 12 cash position after applying monthly net cash movements to the assessment opening bank balance.

Budget alerts highlight a closing funding shortfall, loss-making months and a material deterioration in profit relative to the assessment projection. Alerts are management prompts; they do not prevent saving or submitting the budget.

9.7 Saving and refreshing the projection

Select Save draft after changing budget values, the name or planning notes. The page marks unsaved changes and will not submit them until they are saved.

Select Refresh projection when assessment source records have changed and the comparison baseline must be updated. Refreshing:

  • recalculates the live assessment projection;
  • updates the Projection and Variance views;
  • updates the projection-import date; and
  • does not overwrite any Budget values.
Review before refresh: refresh is available only while the budget is Draft. Record why the source projection changed and review all resulting variances after refreshing.

9.8 Budget workflow

StatusWhat it meansAvailable action
DraftThe budget can be edited and its projection comparison can be refreshed.Save and Submit.
SubmittedThe preparer has completed the draft. Values are read-only while approval is pending.A Tenant Owner or Tenant Administrator may Approve.
ApprovedThe agreed budget is locked as the assessment's approved management plan.A Tenant Owner or Tenant Administrator may Reopen it as Draft when a controlled revision is required.

Every status change is recorded in budget status history with the user and date. Concurrent changes are protected: if another user changes the budget after it was opened, reload the page before saving again.

9.9 Permissions

Tenant roleBudget access
Tenant Owner / Tenant AdministratorCreate, edit and submit authorized assessment budgets; approve and reopen submitted or approved budgets.
Branch ManagerCreate, edit and submit budgets only for assessments within the manager's authorized scope.
ViewerRead budgets for authorized assessments but cannot create, edit, submit, approve or reopen them.

Access is checked by the service for every operation. Hiding a button in the page is not the only security control.

9.10 Recommended budget review

  1. Confirm the budget name, assessment case and Month 1 start date.
  2. Compare Budget with Projection and explain material variances.
  3. Review gross profit, EBITDA and profit before tax by month.
  4. Check that cash receipts and payments are consistent with the profit assumptions.
  5. Review the lowest monthly cash position, not only Month 12 closing cash.
  6. Resolve or document funding shortfalls and loss-making months.
  7. Save the draft, perform a final Projection and Variance review, and then submit.
  8. Approve only after assumptions, evidence, risks and management actions have been reviewed.

9.11 Important limitations

  • The first release supports one active 12-month budget per assessment.
  • The budget is a planning record, not an accounting ledger or replacement for actual management accounts.
  • Budget versus actual reporting is not populated automatically in this release.
  • Editing revenue does not automatically rebuild receipts, VAT, stock purchases or working-capital timing inside the budget.
  • Refreshing the projection preserves budget values by design; users must review the resulting variances.
  • Approving a budget does not change or approve the underlying assessment.
  • Displayed amounts use two decimal places while the database retains four-decimal calculation precision.

10. Registration, Plans & Tenant User Management

Viability.IQ is tenant-based. A tenant is the private Personal or Organisation workspace that owns its clients, businesses, assessments, branches, budgets and user memberships. Application users sign in with one identity account, while their tenant membership determines which workspace records and functions they may use.

Identity versus tenant access: an account proves who the user is. A tenant membership, role, branch and record grants determine what that user may access inside the active tenant. An existing Viability.IQ account can be attached to another tenant without creating a second password.

10.1 Registering a new subscriber

  1. Open Register from the sign-in area. The registration route is /identity/register.
  2. Select the required workspace plan: Standard for an individual or Open Plan for an organisation.
  3. Enter the subscriber's first name, last name, department, job title and province.
  4. Enter the email address and contact details.
  5. Create and confirm a secure password and accept the applicable terms.
  6. For Open Plan, enter the organisation name and initial number of seats.
  7. Submit the registration and sign in after confirmation.

Successful registration creates the Identity account, provisions the tenant and subscription, creates the subscriber's active tenant membership, and assigns the protected Tenant Owner role. If tenant provisioning fails, registration is rolled back rather than leaving an account without its workspace.

Use a unique, controlled email address: the registration email becomes the user's sign-in identity. Verify spelling before submission. Do not share one account among several staff members; create a membership for each person so activity and permissions remain attributable.

10.2 Choosing a plan

PlanWorkspaceSeatsBest suited to
Standard (Individual)Personal tenantOne included seatA sole assessor or individual subscriber who does not need additional active users.
Open PlanOrganisation tenantAt least two seats, with a configurable higher quantityTeams, branches and organisations that need shared access and tenant roles.

The Standard plan is sometimes referred to as the Individual Plan. In the current registration page its displayed name is Standard and its stored plan code is STANDARD. Open Plan uses the code OPEN.

A Standard tenant can be upgraded to Open Plan without moving its tenant data. The subscribed seat quantity must be increased as part of that commercial or administrative process before additional active members can be added.

Current billing status: registrations currently provision the selected subscription as Active. Payment-provider checkout, signed payment webhooks and automatic payment enforcement are not yet part of this release. Confirm the commercial subscription outside the application until billing integration is deployed.

10.3 Understanding seats

A seat represents one active tenant membership, not one role. A member may hold several roles and still use only one seat.

  • The Tenant Owner consumes a seat.
  • Adding a new active member consumes one available seat.
  • Attaching an existing Viability.IQ account as an active member also consumes one seat.
  • Reactivating a suspended or removed member requires an available seat.
  • Suspended or removed memberships do not count as active seats.
  • The Users page shows active members compared with the subscribed seat quantity.

If no seat is available, the application refuses the new or reactivated active membership. Increase the subscription seat quantity or suspend/remove an unused membership before trying again.

10.4 Tenant roles and their purpose

RolePurpose and default operational access

Tenant AdministratorFull operational administration and permission to manage tenant users and role allocations.

10.5 Opening user and role management

Only Tenant Owner and Tenant Administrator can see or use the Security Settings links.

  1. Sign in and open Settings.
  2. Under Security Settings, select Users to open /settings/users.
  3. Select User Groups / Roles to open /settings/roles.
  4. Use the Settings-rooted breadcrumb to move between the administration pages.

User and role forms open in the right-side OffCanvas panel. Save, validation and error outcomes are displayed using application Toast messages.

10.6 Adding a new user

  1. Open Settings > Users and review the active-seat counter.
  2. Select Add user.
  3. Enter the first name, last name and email address.
  4. Select the tenant branch that should be used for branch-oriented access and reporting.
  5. Select one or more tenant roles. Viewer is the initial role selection and may be changed as required.
  6. Save the form.

For a genuinely new Viability.IQ account, the system generates a cryptographically random temporary password, creates the tenant membership and selected roles, and emails the password using the configured SMTP service. The password is never displayed in the page or written to application logs.

If welcome-email delivery fails, creation is rolled back so the tenant is not left with an inaccessible user. Correct the SMTP configuration or recipient address before retrying.

10.7 First sign-in with a temporary password

  1. The new user signs in with the emailed address and temporary password.
  2. Viability.IQ redirects the user to Change Password.
  3. Other application routes remain blocked until the temporary password has been replaced.
  4. After a successful password change, the user can open the tenant functions allowed by the assigned roles and record scope.
Temporary-password handling: send the password only through the configured email workflow. An administrator should never copy it into notes, support tickets or shared chat. If the email is suspected to be compromised, reset the account before use.

10.8 Adding an existing Viability.IQ account

If the email already belongs to a Viability.IQ Identity account, the service attaches that account to the active tenant rather than rejecting it or creating a duplicate identity. The existing password remains valid; no new temporary password is generated.

The tenant administrator must still select the tenant-specific branch and roles. These allocations apply only to the active tenant and do not change the user's membership in another tenant.

10.9 Editing membership, branch and roles

From Settings > Users, select the edit action for a member to update branch, roles and membership status. From User Groups / Roles, select Assign roles when only the role allocation needs to change.

  • Assign the minimum roles necessary for the person's responsibilities.
  • Review multiple-role combinations because permissions accumulate.
  • Use the tenant membership branch rather than the user's global profile branch for operational reporting.
  • The Tenant Owner role is protected and cannot be assigned to another member through these pages.
  • An administrator cannot suspend or remove their own active membership.

10.10 Suspending, reactivating, removing and re-adding

ActionEffectWhen to use it
SuspendStops tenant application access without deleting the membership history and releases the active seat.Temporary leave, investigation or short-term access withdrawal.
ReactivateRestores the membership when a seat is available.The same user returns and should keep the tenant relationship.
RemoveMarks the membership as removed and releases the active seat. Historical identity and tenant records are retained.The user has permanently left the tenant.

Do not delete business or assessment records merely because a member leaves. Record ownership, activity history and explicit record grants remain part of the tenant audit trail.

10.11 Record scope and explicit grants

Tenant isolation prevents users from reading or changing records in another tenant. Within the active tenant:

  • Tenant Owner and Tenant Administrator can work across tenant operational records.
  • Branch Manager and Viewer normally see records created by that user plus records explicitly granted to the membership.
  • Explicit grants may independently allow View, Edit or Delete for an Assessment, Business, Client, Company or Branch.
  • An Assessment grant also governs its child financial records.
  • Billing Manager alone receives no operational-record access.

Membership, tenant and subscription must all be active. Active and Trial subscription statuses are accepted by the authorization service.

10.12 Recommended administration procedure

  1. Confirm that the selected plan and seat quantity match the organisation's approved subscription.
  2. Maintain at least one accessible Tenant Owner account and protect its credentials.
  3. Allocate named accounts; never share user credentials.
  4. Assign the member's branch before allocating operational responsibilities.
  5. Use least privilege and review combinations of multiple roles.
  6. Verify temporary-password delivery and completion of the first password change.
  7. Suspend access promptly when temporary access should stop; remove departed users.
  8. Review active members against paid seats regularly.
  9. Review explicit record grants when responsibilities or branches change.
  10. Test access using representative non-administrator accounts after material role changes.

11. Database Availability, Connection Recovery & Secure Sessions

Viability.IQ uses Blazor Server, which keeps an interactive connection between the browser and the application server. Temporary network interruptions, a sleeping computer, a database restart or an overloaded SQL Server can interrupt that connection without meaning that the user's account or assessment data has been lost.

What is protected: the authenticated user remains governed by the secure authentication cookie. The active tenant membership and assessment identifiers are stored in encrypted browser session storage for the current tab only. After reconnection or a new circuit, Viability.IQ reloads the related information from the database and checks authorization again before restoring the workspace.

11.1 Application startup and database availability

When Viability.IQ starts, it verifies the Identity and application databases and applies outstanding migrations. A temporary SQL Server connection failure is retried up to five times, with a five-second delay between attempts.

  • The web server starts before database initialization, so the browser receives a branded Service temporarily unavailable page instead of appearing to hang.
  • While initialization or a retry is in progress, the page reports the current attempt and refreshes automatically.
  • If the database becomes available during the retry period, startup continues normally.
  • If every startup attempt fails, the web server remains online only to display the database-unavailable page. Login and all normal application routes remain blocked.
  • The health endpoint returns HTTP 503 while the database is unavailable and HTTP 200 after initialization succeeds.
  • The full technical exception is recorded through Serilog for support and diagnostics.
  • Database credentials and raw SQL exception details are not displayed to end users.
Administrator action: if startup repeatedly fails, confirm that SQL Server is running, the configured database is reachable, the application identity has permission, and the connection string targets the intended environment. Restart the application after correcting the underlying problem.

11.2 Database errors during normal use

During normal use, connection attempts and read operations use controlled timeouts and transient connection retries. The standard settings allow 15 seconds to establish a connection and 60 seconds for a command to complete.

User messageMeaningRecommended response
Database TimeoutThe database did not complete the operation within the configured period.Wait briefly and select Try again. If it repeats, record the error reference and contact support.
Database Temporarily UnavailableA transient connection, network or SQL Server availability problem was detected.Allow the service time to recover, then retry the page or operation.
Database Operation FailedThe operation failed for a database reason that should not be automatically repeated.Do not repeatedly submit a write operation. Give support the displayed error reference.

Each global error message includes an error reference that can be matched to the Serilog and detailed error records. Write operations are not blindly repeated because doing so could duplicate an insert, payment, movement or other transaction when the original result is uncertain.

11.3 Blazor Server connection recovery

The server sends a connection keep-alive every 15 seconds and allows up to 60 seconds without a client response before treating the connection as interrupted. A disconnected circuit is retained for three minutes to allow the same browser tab to reconnect without immediately discarding its in-memory component state.

A brief reconnection does not count as user inactivity and does not itself sign the user out. If the interruption lasts beyond the retained-circuit period, Viability.IQ creates a new circuit and restores the protected per-tab context.

11.4 Browser Back, Forward, refresh and restored context

Browser navigation can recreate a Blazor circuit, particularly after a full page load, refresh, external navigation or an expired disconnected circuit. Viability.IQ protects the minimum context needed to continue:

  • authenticated user identifier;
  • active tenant membership identifier; and
  • active assessment identifier, when an assessment is open.

The information is stored only for the current browser tab. Another tab establishes its own context. On restoration, Viability.IQ confirms that the signed-in user still owns the stored tenant membership, that the membership and subscription remain active, and that the assessment remains accessible. It then reloads tenant, business, client, assessment and data-status details from the database.

Authorization always wins: protected browser storage is a continuity aid, not an authorization source. If a role, membership, subscription or record grant has changed, the old context is rejected or cleared rather than restored.

11.5 Inactivity warning and automatic logout

A signed-in session closes automatically after 30 minutes without user interaction. Five minutes before logout, a Toast warns that the session is expiring.

  • Mouse, keyboard, touch and scroll activity reset the inactivity timer.
  • Background server processing or an open page with no user interaction does not reset the timer.
  • At the timeout, the browser performs a secure sign-out and returns to Login.
  • The Login page confirms that the previous session expired because of inactivity.
  • The authentication cookie is removed before logout auditing is attempted, so a database outage cannot keep an inactive user signed in.

After signing in again, return to the required assessment and confirm any unsaved form values. Session restoration preserves navigation context; it does not save partially entered form data that was never submitted.

11.6 Closing a tab, using several tabs and shared computers

  • Tenant and assessment context is isolated per browser tab and is not intentionally shared between tabs.
  • Closing a tab removes access to that tab's protected session storage when the browser clears the tab session.
  • The authentication cookie may still apply to another open tab until inactivity timeout or explicit sign-out.
  • On a shared computer, always use Sign Out; do not rely only on closing the browser window.
  • Do not use browser Back or Forward to assume that an unsaved edit was committed. Confirm the success Toast before leaving a form.

11.8 Troubleshooting checklist

  1. Note the time, page, action and displayed error reference.
  2. Determine whether the message describes database availability, Blazor reconnection or inactivity.
  3. For a connection interruption, wait for automatic reconnection before using Reload.
  4. After Reload or browser Back/Forward, confirm the displayed tenant and assessment before continuing.
  5. For repeated database timeouts, inspect the Serilog files and SQL Server health rather than continually increasing timeout values.
  6. For automatic logout, sign in again and verify whether the previous form operation showed a success Toast before the timeout.
  7. Escalate persistent incidents with the error reference and relevant log period; never send passwords or connection-string secrets.
